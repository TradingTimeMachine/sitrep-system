MODEL IMPACT: COMPLICATES, summit breakthrough probability revised downward
The Issue 004 thesis assessed the Trump-Xi summit as the primary resolution mechanism with a 30-35% probability of producing a Hormuz deal. Today's White House expectation management, combined with Iran's "never bow" posture and Kuwait attack, revises that probability downward to 15-20%. The structural thesis remains unchanged: the IRGC controls Iranian state decision-making, the civilian track cannot produce binding commitments, and the decoupling proposal remains Iran's actual minimum. What has changed is the summit's capacity to bridge that gap. A summit that both sides are entering with lowered expectations and Iran simultaneously hardening its formal position is not the architecture for a breakthrough.
On the Kuwait attack: the expansion of IRGC paramilitary operations to Kuwait marks a qualitative escalation in GCC direct exposure. The UAE attack on May 4 was framed by Iran as defensive. A IRGC paramilitary team dispatched to Kuwait to conduct an attack is offensive in character. If this pattern continues, it will force GCC states to make a choice between formal military alignment with the U.S. and accommodation of Iran's regional dominance claim. That is a choice the GCC has been deferring since the ceasefire began.
On Iran's BRICS parallel track: Araghchi at the BRICS Foreign Ministers meeting in Delhi simultaneously with Trump in Beijing is Iran demonstrating that it has an alternative diplomatic architecture to offer beyond the U.S.-Pakistan-China track. BRICS represents the non-Western coalition Iran has been cultivating throughout the crisis. The parallel timing is deliberate.
