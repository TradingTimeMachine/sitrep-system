MODEL IMPACT: COMPLICATES with escalation risk elevation
The Issue 002 thesis identified the IRGC permission regime as an emerging structural goal and assessed the indefinite ceasefire as delivering reconstitution time without pressure. Today's shoot-to-kill order partially disrupts that assessment. The U.S. has reintroduced a credible military threat within the ceasefire framework rather than simply extending it passively. This does not falsify the thesis: the IRGC still controls Iranian state decision-making, the civilian track still cannot produce binding commitments, and reconstitution continues. But the mechanism has shifted. The ceasefire is no longer a pure IRGC gift. It now contains an active U.S. military component that raises the cost of IRGC mine-laying operations directly.
The critical complication is the dual sovereignty claim. Iran's parliament speaker Ghalibaf said his country would not reopen the Strait of Hormuz as long as the U.S. naval blockade remained, stating a full ceasefire "only makes sense if it is not violated by the naval blockade." report The IRGC claims the right to authorize Hormuz transit. Trump claims the U.S. Navy authorizes Hormuz transit. These two claims are now being asserted simultaneously with shoot-to-kill orders backing the U.S. side. The probability of an inadvertent kinetic incident between U.S. and IRGC forces in the strait has risen materially today. That incident, if it occurs, would collapse the ceasefire immediately regardless of diplomatic track status.
