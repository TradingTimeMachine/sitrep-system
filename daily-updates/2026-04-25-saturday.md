GLOBAL INPUT CHECK
The most significant global input today is American, not regional. The U.S. Treasury sanctioned Hengli Petrochemical in Dalian, China's second-largest independent refinery, for purchasing Iranian crude oil, while simultaneously issuing secondary sanctions warnings to China, the UAE, Oman, and Hong Kong for doing business with Iran. This lands three weeks before the scheduled Trump-Xi summit, forcing Beijing into a visible choice between its Iran oil supply chain and its Washington relationship at the worst possible diplomatic moment. Brent crude oil rose above $105 per barrel today as Hormuz uncertainty continues to weigh on medium-term market outlook. On the Levant track, Israel and Lebanon extended their ceasefire by three weeks, which analytically removes the Lebanon precondition Iran has been using as a stalling mechanism for another cycle.

SIGNAL OF THE DAY
The Hengli Petrochemical sanctions are a structural escalation in the U.S. pressure campaign, not a tactical one. Washington has moved from interdicting shadow fleet vessels at sea to sanctioning named Chinese industrial infrastructure on Chinese soil, directly targeting the demand side of Iran's oil revenue rather than the supply side. This is analytically significant because it begins to close the ghost fleet circumvention pathway that has allowed Iran to remain economically pressured rather than economically isolated, and it does so by making the cost of Iranian oil purchases fall on Beijing rather than on Tehran.

MODEL IMPACT: PIVOT
The Issue 002 thesis assessed the blockade as primarily a maritime enforcement operation with a ghost fleet leakage problem. Today's action pivots the U.S. pressure mechanism from maritime interdiction to financial strangulation targeting the buyer side of Iran's oil network. The structural diagnosis remains unchanged: the IRGC controls Iranian state decision-making, the civilian track cannot produce binding commitments, and reconstitution continues. But the mechanism through which maximum pressure is being applied has upgraded materially. The blockade alone was producing attrition. The Hengli sanctions combined with secondary sanctions warnings to sovereign states begin to produce isolation. Those are different instruments with different timelines and different effects on Iranian decision calculus.
The critical complication is that Hengli and similar teapot refineries have limited exposure to the U.S. financial system, which partially insulates them from the full sanctions effect. Sanctioning Chinese banks that facilitate the purchases would be the next and qualitatively more severe rung on this ladder. That step has not been taken yet, but the warning letters sent to Chinese banks earlier this month signal Washington is prepared to climb it. The Trump-Xi summit in a few weeks is now directly entangled with the Iran pressure campaign in a way it was not three days ago.
On the Islamabad talks: Iran has still not confirmed attendance for the expected second round. The civilian foreign ministry continues to signal refusal publicly while Vance prepares to travel. The dual-track thesis holds without adjustment.
