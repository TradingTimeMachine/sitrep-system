GLOBAL INPUT CHECK
The Trump-Xi summit has produced a joint statement with nine specific commitments, materially exceeding the "thin deliverables" base case the Issue 005 forecast assessed at 50-55% probability. Xi agreed with Trump that Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon, expressed opposition to the militarization of Hormuz and any effort to charge a toll for its use, asserted that the Strait must remain open for free energy flows, and told Trump that China would not provide military equipment to Iran. Trump described Xi's offer to help broker peace with Iran as "a big statement." Xi also expressed interest in purchasing more American oil to reduce China's dependence on the Strait. This is a structural signal that must be read alongside the Iran commitments. The Taiwan-for-Iran trade-off identified as a risk variable in Issue 005 has partially materialized, not as a formal concession but as a sequencing arrangement in which China gave Trump an Iran win and immediately cashed it for Taiwan pressure. MODEL IMPACT: PIVOT
The Issue 005 thesis identified the summit's most likely outcome as thin deliverables at 50-55% probability, with a private Chinese commitment scenario at 20-25%. The actual outcome exceeds the private commitment scenario: China has made public commitments on Hormuz toll opposition, military equipment denial, and nuclear agreement. This is a PIVOT in the pressure mechanism, not a falsification of the structural thesis. The IRGC still controls Iranian state decision-making. Mojtaba is still absent. The civilian track still cannot produce binding commitments. But the external pressure architecture has materially upgraded: China has now publicly aligned with U.S. Hormuz and nuclear positions, which removes Beijing's ability to maintain its passive beneficiary posture without visible contradiction.
The critical analytical question is whether China's public commitments translate into actual behavioral change toward Iran, specifically whether Chinese banks stop lending to sanctioned Iranian refiners and whether Chinese-flagged vessels stop accepting Iranian oil cargoes. Public statements are not pressure. Financial behavior is pressure. The Issue 005 thesis specified that the analytically relevant signal would be observable within 2-4 weeks in Chinese bank behavior and Iranian oil purchase volumes, not in summit communiques.
On the Taiwan dimension: Xi's warning is the most significant structural development of the summit for the broader geopolitical picture. China delivered Iran cooperation and immediately demanded Taiwan reciprocity. Whether Trump signed off on the $14 billion Taiwan arms package, which was sitting on his desk, is the critical follow-on question. If Trump delayed or declined the Taiwan arms sale in exchange for China's Iran cooperation, the summit produced a Taiwan concession that has not yet been reported publicly but will become observable within weeks.
