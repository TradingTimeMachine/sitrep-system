MODEL IMPACT
The Lebanon ceasefire complicates the baseline thesis in one specific way: it removes one of Iran's stated preconditions for further negotiation, which the baseline assessed as structurally inoperative. If Iran's negotiating posture was genuinely conditioned on Lebanon and not simply a stalling mechanism, today's development should produce visible movement toward a second round of talks within the next 48-72 hours. If it does not - if Iran finds a new precondition or stays silent - that reinforces the thesis that the IRGC has no interest in a deal and is using diplomacy as a reorganization interval. The U.S. and Iran are also now reportedly considering a two-week ceasefire extension to allow more time to negotiate Bloomberg, which is consistent with the baseline pattern of extending rather than resolving. The blockade meanwhile is delivering real pressure: Pentagon officials said Thursday the operation has disrupted roughly 90% of Iran's maritime trade activity. WDTV The structural problem remains unchanged - economic pressure is real, but the entity that would need to authorize a deal cannot constitutionally do so.
